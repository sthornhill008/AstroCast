Glossary of Terms

Planetary condition and rulership

  • Domicile, detriment, and peregrine. Domicile is a planet’s home sign. Detriment is the sign opposite a home sign. Peregrine denotes a placement credited with no sign-based dignity under the export’s convention. Eleanor’s Venus is in domicile, Daniel’s Mercury is in detriment, and both Moons are peregrine.
  • Under the beams. A traditional condition of reduced visibility within roughly 8°–17° of the Sun. It applies to Eleanor’s Venus and Daniel’s Mercury.
  • Sect and hayz. Sect is the division into daytime and nighttime planetary teams. Hayz adds favorable alignment by hemisphere and sign gender. Eleanor’s Saturn and Daniel’s Mercury qualify in their natal charts.
  • In-sect benefic and out-of-sect malefic. Traditional terms for a planet offering support within the chart’s team and the planet of challenge working outside it. Jupiter and Mars, respectively, fill these roles in both day charts.
  • Mutual reception and triplicity. Two planets are in reception when each occupies a sign where the other has dignity. Triplicity gives rulership by element. Daniel’s Venus and Jupiter share a domicile reception, and Daniel’s Sun and Venus share a day-triplicity reception.
  • Final dispositor or loop. Follow each planet to the ruler of its sign. Eleanor’s chains lead to Venus, while Daniel’s end in the Venus–Jupiter exchange.

Contacts and life areas

  • Conjunction, opposition, square, trine, and sextile. Zodiacal contacts measured from 0°, 180°, 90°, 120°, and 60°, respectively. The orb is the distance from exactness. A cross-chart contact compares fixed natal positions.
  • Semisquare and quincunx. A 45-degree friction aspect and a 150-degree adjustment aspect. The Venus–Moon semisquare and Pluto–Chiron quincunx are especially tight in this comparison.
  • House overlay and angles. An overlay places one person’s planet in the other’s chart and describes the life area receiving the emphasis. The Ascendant, Descendant, Midheaven, and IC mark the eastern horizon, western horizon, upper meridian, and lower meridian in these charts.
  • Part of Marriage. A calculated zodiacal point used here to explore how partnership is understood and negotiated.
  • Waning gibbous. The lunar phase between the full Moon and last quarter. Both charts place the Moon in the middle of that phase, associated here with revisiting and sharing experience.

Additional ways of comparing contacts

  • Declination, parallel, and contraparallel. Declination measures distance north or south of the celestial equator. A parallel joins similar positions on the same side, and a contraparallel matches them across opposite sides. Eleanor’s Saturn parallels Daniel’s Jupiter and is contraparallel to Daniel’s Neptune.
  • Out of bounds and station. A body is out of bounds when its declination exceeds the chart’s solar limit. A station is where apparent zodiacal motion changes direction. Eleanor’s Uranus has both emphases near the birth time.
  • Antiscia. Zodiacal positions mirrored across the solstice axis. Eleanor’s Venus and Daniel’s Ascendant form this contact by their supplied longitudes.
  • Midpoint. The halfway point between two chart positions. Eleanor’s Pluto contacts Eleanor’s Sun/Moon midpoint, while Daniel’s Mars contacts Daniel’s North Node/Midheaven midpoint.
  • Paran. A pairing of local horizon or meridian crossings, calculated for one birthplace at a time. Daniel’s natal Moon–Venus paran is calculated for Albany. Eleanor’s natal Venus–Uranus paran is calculated for Harrisburg.
  • Fixed-star reactivation. A cross-chart contact to a planet or angle that already carries a natal star conjunction. For example, Daniel’s Neptune contacts Eleanor’s Ascendant, which carries Unukalhai.
