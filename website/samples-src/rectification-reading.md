### How the dates were tested

The event list spans bereavement, military service, public work, partnership, parenthood and public decisions. For each event, I reviewed the listed directions within 365 days of the event, sought a plausible symbolic match and gave the angles priority. Stars, mirrors and broad themes get less evidentiary weight. One working match per event enters the comparison. Plausible competing matches and alternate milestone dates show how the result changes with those choices.

The event gap is the documented calendar date minus the exported direction date. Divide the gap in days by 30.436875 to get months, then divide by the signed Months/min value. A positive result moves the birth time later. AstroCast's printed ±6-minute ranges screen the candidates. The shifts are local estimates from rounded rates and day-level dates.

## What the dates can establish

For trial shifts of −1, −0.5, 0, +0.5 and +1 minute, the mean absolute gaps across the same ten matches are 124.4, 110.6, 115.6, 135.2, 163.6 days, respectively. This simple residual check holds the pairings and slopes fixed. The best of these trials improves on the recorded time by about 5 days per event. The estimated shift also changes when the event definition changes, as the launch and opening dates for Invictus illustrate.

The matches were chosen retrospectively from many available directions. Several depend on broad symbolism, and milestones in the same transition share context. The offsets are descriptive and provide no statistical confidence interval. Two dates of the same episode count as one event. The supplied export's natal chart, direction dates and sensitivities are the calculation basis.

**The recorded 16:20 BST remains the best-supported working time.**
